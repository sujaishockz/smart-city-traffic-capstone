## Annual Traffic Trends (2012–2017)

| Year | Hours Recorded | Total Traffic Volume | Avg Hourly Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2012 | 2,559 | 8,208,767 | 3,207.8 |
| 2013 | 8,573 | 28,177,412 | 3,286.8 |
| 2014 | 4,839 | 15,731,289 | 3,250.9 |
| 2015 | 4,373 | 14,181,206 | 3,242.9 |
| 2016 | 9,306 | 29,494,821 | 3,169.4 |
| 2017 | 10,605 | 35,428,156 | 3,340.7 |

Year-on-year change in **total** traffic volume:

| Year | Total Volume | Change vs Prior Year | % Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2013 | 28,177,412 | +19,968,645 | +243.3% |
| 2014 | 15,731,289 | -12,446,123 | -44.2% |
| 2015 | 14,181,206 | -1,550,083 | -9.9% |
| 2016 | 29,494,821 | +15,313,615 | +108.0% |
| 2017 | 35,428,156 | +5,933,335 | +20.1% |

**Observations:**
1. Large swings in *total* yearly volume (e.g. +243% in 2013, -44% in 2014)
   are driven almost entirely by how many hours were recorded that year
   (`hours_recorded` ranges from 2,559 to 10,605), not by an actual change in
   traffic demand — 2013, 2014 and 2015 all have large data gaps
   (e.g. 2015 only has recorded data from June onward). **Average hourly
   traffic volume is therefore the more reliable trend indicator.**
2. Using average hourly volume, traffic is fairly stable in the
   ~3,170–3,340 vehicles/hour range across all six years, with a mild dip in
   2016 (3,169.4) followed by the highest average of the period in 2017
   (3,340.7) — consistent with a gradually recovering/growing corridor rather
   than a dramatic shift.
3. Any year-over-year comparison of *totals* for this dataset must be caveated
   with the uneven data coverage per year; this will be addressed by
   resampling/aggregating on a common basis (e.g. average per hour-of-day) in
   later analysis.

## Temperature Around Holidays (2015–2017)

| Year | Holiday | Hours Recorded | Avg Temp (K) | Avg Temp (°C) | Avg Traffic Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | New Year's Day | 19 | 267.03 | -6.12 | 1,815.2 |
| 2017 | New Year's Day | 24 | 270.09 | -3.06 | 2,127.6 |
| 2015 | Labor Day | 24 | 295.32 | 22.17 | 2,430.3 |
| 2016 | Labor Day | 40 | 294.75 | 21.60 | 1,868.3 |
| 2017 | Labor Day | 28 | 290.63 | 17.48 | 2,525.7 |

*(2015 New Year's Day has no rows in the dataset — 2015 data only starts from
June onward, another instance of the coverage gap noted above.)*

**Observations:**
1. New Year's Day 2017 was noticeably milder than 2016 (-3.1°C vs -6.1°C, a
   +3.1°C difference) and also saw higher average traffic (2,127.6 vs 1,815.2
   vehicles), consistent with the idea that milder holiday weather is
   associated with more people out driving.
2. Labor Day 2017 was cooler than 2015/2016 (17.5°C vs ~22°C) yet had the
   *highest* average traffic volume of the three years — suggesting Labor Day
   traffic (a "back to routine" day after a long weekend) is more strongly
   driven by the day-of-week/commute pattern than by temperature, unlike New
   Year's Day which falls on a fixed calendar date regardless of weekday.
3. Because holiday traffic volume is also a function of which day of the week
   the holiday fell on in each year, temperature alone does not fully explain
   the year-to-year differences — this is a useful segue into the upcoming
   correlation analysis.
